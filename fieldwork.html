Fieldwork &
Expeditions

Six major field campaigns across Kenya's montane forests, savanna mosaics, wetlands, and national parks — spanning over 5,900 small mammal specimens collected in collaboration with the National Museums of Kenya and the Kunming Institute of Zoology.

5,974 Total specimens
6 Field campaigns
12+ Ecosystems sampled
10+ Years of fieldwork

All field surveys are conducted as joint collaborations between the National Museums of Kenya and the Kunming Institute of Zoology, Chinese Academy of Sciences — a Sino-African partnership that combines local ecological expertise with genomic and systematic research capacity.

Sampling employs live-trapping with Sherman and pitfall traps, habitat suitability analysis, and strategic transect design across elevational gradients and ecoregional contrasts. Morphological measurements, tissue extraction, and voucher specimen preparation are conducted in situ. Specimens are accessioned into the National Museums of Kenya mammal collection.

Mount Kenya — Chogoria & Sirimon Transects

Elevational survey of East Africa's second-highest peak, conducted along two transects (Chogoria on the south-east slope and Sirimon on the north-west). Sampling covered habitats from montane forest through bamboo zone, moorland, to alpine heath — generating the dataset underpinning several key publications on elevational diversity gradients. This remains the largest single-site small mammal dataset collected from Kenya.

Field Methods

Sampling Protocols

🪤
Live Trapping

Sherman live-traps and pitfall-with-drift-fence arrays deployed along elevational transects at standardised spacing. Traps set at dusk and checked at dawn over 3–5 consecutive nights per site.

📐
Morphometrics

Standardised external measurements (head-body, tail, ear, hindfoot) and cranial measurements taken in situ. Photographs and tissue samples (ear biopsy in 95% ethanol) taken from all individuals.

🧬
Tissue & Voucher Preparation

Voucher specimens prepared as dry skins with skulls and accessioned into the National Museums of Kenya mammal collection (NMK). Tissue samples archived at NMK and KIZ, CAS for subsequent genomic analyses.
