Systematics & Integrative Taxonomy

Phylogenomics UCE Morphometrics

I use multi-locus molecular data, morphometrics, and ultraconserved elements (UCEs) to resolve species boundaries, reconstruct phylogenies, and describe cryptic diversity within African small mammal groups — particularly murid rodents and insectivores.

A major focus has been the genus Lophuromys (brush-furred rats) across equatorial Africa, where integrative approaches have revealed several previously unrecognised lineages with important biogeographic and conservation implications. Recent UCE-based work on African dormice (Graphiurus) adds a deeper temporal dimension to understanding diversification across sub-Saharan Africa.

Collaborative work has extended to Asian shrews (Chodsigoa, Anourosorex), moles (Talpidae), and hedgehogs (Mesechinus) within the Kunming Institute of Zoology group, developing expertise in integrative taxonomy across taxonomic groups and continents.

Macroecology & Diversity Patterns

Biogeography Spatial ecology Functional diversity

A parallel thread of my research asks how species richness, functional diversity, and phylogenetic diversity vary across environmental gradients — and why. I examine how spatial scale mediates the patterns and processes structuring small mammal communities.

Using datasets from transects across Kenya's montane ecosystems (Mt. Kenya, Mt. Elgon, Cherang'any Hills), I analyse how climate, topography, and land use interact at multiple spatial grains to produce the diversity patterns we observe. This work has practical relevance for understanding which factors to target in conservation prioritisation.

A key finding from this programme is that the relationship between diversity and environment is strongly scale-dependent: predictors that explain macroecological patterns at coarse grains often lose explanatory power at finer resolutions, and vice versa. This has fundamental implications for how we generalise from field data to broad conservation inferences.

Conservation Biology

Applied ecology Protected areas Genomics

I evaluate the management effectiveness of Kenya's protected area network using mammal diversity indices as proxies for ecosystem health, identifying spatial mismatches between biodiversity hotspots and conservation designations.

My current NSFC-funded project focuses on conservation genomics of the Lophuromys flavopunctatus species group — examining how population structure, gene flow, and adaptive variation can inform management of this rodent complex, which also has relevance to human welfare through zoonotic disease risk.

I also engage with broader conservation questions around human–wildlife coexistence, functional homogenization outside protected areas, and the spatiotemporal distribution of mammal communities in biodiversity hotspots. My fieldwork in Kenya provides empirical grounding for modelling and policy-relevant outputs.

Current Funding

NSFC 2024

Active Research Grant

National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC)
Grant No. 32350410430 · Jan–Dec 2024 · ~$33,300 USD

Genomic analysis of the Lophuromys flavopunctatus species group: relevancy of conservation genomics to enhancing biodiversity management and human welfare in East Africa.

This project integrates population-level genomics with landscape ecology to understand how habitat fragmentation and land-use change shape genetic diversity across protected and unprotected areas in Kenya. The work has applied implications for managing the zoonotic risks associated with this rodent group, which is implicated in the transmission of haemorrhagic fever viruses across East Africa.
