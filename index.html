Academic Research

Research
Programme

My research bridges molecular ecology, evolutionary biology, and conservation science — using Africa's small mammals as a window into biodiversity processes, historical biogeography, and the drivers of community assembly.

01

Systematics & Integrative Taxonomy

Phylogenomics

I use multi-locus molecular data, morphometrics, and ultraconserved elements (UCEs) to resolve species boundaries, reconstruct phylogenies, and describe cryptic diversity within African small mammal groups — particularly murid rodents and insectivores.

A major focus has been the genus Lophuromys (brush-furred rats) across equatorial Africa, where integrative approaches have revealed several previously unrecognised lineages with important biogeographic and conservation implications. Recent UCE-based work on African dormice (Graphiurus) adds a deeper temporal dimension to understanding diversification.

Lophuromys Graphiurus Lemniscomys Crocidura Micromys Chodsigoa
02

Macroecology & Diversity Patterns

Biogeography

A parallel thread of my research asks how species richness, functional diversity, and phylogenetic diversity vary across environmental gradients — and why. I examine how spatial scale mediates the patterns and processes structuring small mammal communities.

Using datasets from transects across Kenya's montane ecosystems (Mt. Kenya, Mt. Elgon, Cherang'any Hills), I analyse how climate, topography, and land use interact at multiple spatial grains to produce the diversity patterns we observe. This work has practical relevance for understanding which factors to target in conservation prioritisation.

Elevational gradients Beta-diversity Functional traits Niche theory
03

Conservation Biology

Applied Ecology

I evaluate the management effectiveness of Kenya's protected area network using mammal diversity indices as proxies for ecosystem health, identifying spatial mismatches between biodiversity hotspots and conservation designations.

My current NSFC-funded project focuses on conservation genomics of the Lophuromys flavopunctatus species group — examining how population structure, gene flow, and adaptive variation can inform management of this rodent complex, which also has relevance to human welfare through zoonotic disease risk.

Protected areas Conservation genomics Zoonotic risk Climate vulnerability
04

Current Funding

NSFC 2024

My active research grant from the National Natural Science Foundation of China (NSFC) (Grant No. 32350410430; 2024–2024; ~$33,300 USD) supports genomic analysis of the Lophuromys flavopunctatus species group, examining the relevancy of conservation genomics to enhancing biodiversity management and human welfare in East Africa.

This project integrates population-level genomics with landscape ecology to understand how habitat fragmentation and land-use change shape genetic diversity across protected and unprotected areas in Kenya.

Empirical Foundation

Fieldwork &
Expeditions

Six major fieldwork campaigns across Kenya's montane forests, savanna mosaics, wetlands, and national parks — spanning over 5,900 small mammal specimens collected in collaboration with the National Museums of Kenya and the Kunming Institute of Zoology.

5,974 Total specimens
6 Field campaigns
12+ Ecosystems sampled
10+ Years of fieldwork

All field surveys are conducted as joint collaborations between the National Museums of Kenya and the Kunming Institute of Zoology, Chinese Academy of Sciences — a Sino-African partnership that combines local ecological expertise with genomic and systematic research capacity.

Sampling employs live-trapping, habitat suitability analysis, and strategic transect design across elevational gradients and ecoregional contrasts, with morphological measurements, tissue extraction, and voucher specimen preparation conducted in situ.
